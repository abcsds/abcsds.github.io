Understanding and detecting human emotions is crucial for enhancing mental health, cognitive performance and human–computer interactions. The main objective of this study was to investigate the influence of external stimulation, specifically different light conditions, on brain activity while observing affect-eliciting pictures, and on their classification. Multichannel electroencephalography (EEG) was recorded in 30 participants as they observed images from the Nencki Affective Picture System (NAPS) database in an art-gallery-style Virtual Reality (VR) environment. The elicited affect states were classified into three affect classes within the two-dimensional valence–arousal plane, with valence and arousal reported by participants on continuous scales. The experiment was conducted under a warm light condition and a cold light condition, giving three classification tasks: affect state under warm light, affect state under cold light, and warm versus cold light during observation of affect-eliciting images.
